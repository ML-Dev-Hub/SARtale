# Winners answer one question; SARtale needs one

Across 30 NASA Space Apps global winners from 2023 to 2025, the shared pattern is **one named audience, one clear question, and one thing a viewer remembers**. That thing was delivered as a demo of about 30 seconds and built on a small number of named NASA datasets. Analytical depth decided only the Science, Data and Technology awards. **No SAR-challenge entry has ever won a global award.** The best SAR results were Global Finalist (SARcologists in 2023, TengriSpacers in 2025), and both advanced on audience design and a high-stakes local story more than on radar processing. Judged against that field, SARtale (team SARcastic Scientists, "Dancing with the SARs", Islamabad Local Event) has science and relevance that are rare among SAR entries. It is the only entry in the record that processes real NISAR data, tests its change layer against shuffled dates, and gives "what the satellite missed" a screen of its own. Its weak points are the ones winners do not have. Its user and outcome are hypothetical, and nothing exists yet that a judge can open: no deployed app, no public repo, no deck, and a 5:06 methods video where every measured 2025 winner used 29 to 30.5 seconds. Above all of this sits an unresolved eligibility question about work done before the 14 November start, which both the internal audit and Codex's independent review rank as the top risk. The highest-value work for a two-person team is therefore packaging and framing, not more radar. That means getting a ruling, shipping a judge-openable app, an app-first short video and a short deck, turning the ending into a task a visitor performs, and tightening a handful of overreaching phrases. The rules behind several of these choices, including the 30-second limit, are inferred until the 2026 Submission and Judging guides publish on 13 November 2026.

## Thirty winners, one pattern: a specific user and a 30-second hook

The 2025 funnel shows how hard selection is: **11,500+ submissions, about 1,290 Global Nominees, 45 Global Finalists plus 23 Honorable Mentions, and 10 winners**, about 0.09% of submissions ([NASA 2025](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/); [Global Finalists](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/global-finalists/); [Honorable Mentions](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/honorable-mentions/)). Local judges pick nominees, NASA and partner subject-matter experts pick finalists, and an executive committee assigns the ten awards ([Awards 2025](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/)). The award set was identical across all three years, so the table below compares like with like. Sources are the three NASA announcements ([2023](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners), [2024](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/), [2025](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/)) and each team's project page. Product descriptions are the teams' own claims.

Three features recur. The first is **a human-scale anchor**. QUEÑARIS tied its platform to Arequipa's water supply and 5,000 prepared seedlings ([QUEÑARIS](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/watana-project/)). G.R.O.W. opened with "Gabriel", a coffee farmer who lost 10 acres to fire a day after four FIRMS detections ([Team I.O.](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183757/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/team-io/?tab=project)). PureFlow reduced its whole pitch to "Would Your Habitat Survive a Solar Storm... Today?" ([PureFlow](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/pureflow2/)). Henry's Farm Adventure, which is directly relevant to a Pakistani team, includes **Pakistan's 2022 floods** as one of its five farm stops ([Zumorroda-X](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/zumorroda-x/)).

The second is that **Earth-observation winners turned one variable into a plain-language status or action**: an irrigation schedule labelled "Hydrated" or "Dehydrated", a planting window, a weekly fire count ([GaamaRamma](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183805/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/?tab=project); [NVS-knot](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183831/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/nvs-knot/?tab=project)). None ended on a raw raster.

The third is that **storytelling winners used a character or role frame, a chaptered path, and an interactive check at the end**, on one to four datasets with simple analysis. Examples are TerraTales' "Mother Earth", the alien recipient in Greetings from Earth!!, and the AI museum host in Connected Earth Museum ([TerraTales](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183834/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/terratales/?tab=project); [Greetings](https://web.archive.org/web/20240126182158/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/greetings-from-earth1/?tab=project)). The 2025 Storytelling winner processed no dataset at all. It cited NASA and NOAA reference pages ([HerCode Space](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/hercode-space/)). A story on real processed data therefore has room to beat that precedent.

The demos were short. **All five measurable 2025 winner videos ran 29 to 30.5 seconds, and the two PDF demos ran 6 and 7 pages.** In 2023 and 2024, **11 of 15 measured YouTube demos ran 27 to 31 seconds**, and TeamVoyagers titled theirs "30 Seconds of Glory" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mtHMyOQ0vzA)). There were exceptions, though. WMPGang won Best Use of Science with a 93-second video, Asteroid Destroyer used 45 seconds and Storm Prophet 41 ([WMPGang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RBl_3ZsmVFo); [Asteroid Destroyer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SYPQo4-P5J0); [Storm Prophet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NXp3sfRW8tk)). Polish was not a prerequisite either. Astro Sweepers' demo was a file named "WhatsApp Video…", and its page kept assistant-draft phrasing such as "which judges value" ([Astro Sweepers](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/astro-sweepers-we-catch-what-space-leaves-behind/)). Winners openly disclosed generative tools, including Lovable, Bolt, ChatGPT, Gemini and ElevenLabs. **Working code was common but not universal.** Twelve of the 20 winners from 2023–24 had both a deployed URL and a public repo, while only three of the ten 2025 winners surfaced a repo.

## No SAR entry has won; the best stopped at finalist on story, not depth

Before 2026 there were two SAR challenges. **2023's "Create a Work of SARt"** was an art brief judged on "creativity, innovation, and clarity", with 73 submissions ([2023 SARt](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/challenges/create-a-work-of-sart-synthetic-aperture-radar-art/)). **2025's "Through the Radar Looking Glass"** was an interpretation brief requiring multiple frequencies or polarisations, with 200 submissions ([2025 challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/challenges/through-the-radar-looking-glass-revealing-earth-processes-with-sar/)). 2024 had none. A keyword scan of all 30 global winners found no SAR, Sentinel-1, NISAR, UAVSAR or InSAR content.

The strongest SAR results were these. In 2023, **SARcologists (USA)** became a Global Finalist with a VEX IQ robot model of the NISAR spacecraft that processed no imagery ([team](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/girl-power/)). SARtists and Scientist-Astronauts took Honorable Mentions with a time-lapse art piece and QR-coded postcards ([SARtists](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/sartists/); [Scientist-Astronauts](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/scientist-astronauts/)). In 2025, **TengriSpacers (Kyrgyzstan)** became a Global Finalist with "an APP (no UI)" for Sentinel-1 glacier change, framed around glaciers as national water, hydropower and outburst-flood risk. Its README describes different methods from its project text and gives no validation ([team](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/tengrispacers/); [repo](https://github.com/Apelsinovich/GlacierSAR-Kyrgyzstan)). **FarmHubGlobal (France)** took an Honorable Mention with a Lovable-built farmer app that names one Earthdata collection and no processing steps ([team](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/farmhubglobal/)). In 2025, 36 SAR-challenge projects were nominated, and **only these two advanced**.

The technically deepest 2025 entries all stopped at nominee. These were Arapua Labs (Otsu on linear VV, dune displacement), SAR Monsters (L-band for flooded vegetation, IoU and precision/recall validation), UpIT (8-year PS-InSAR), Surface Sleuths (stated single-track and C-band limits) and Yekta (150 scenes) ([Arapua Labs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/arapua-labs/); [SAR Monsters](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/sar-monsters/); [UpIT](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/upit/); [Surface Sleuths](https://github.com/Yasaswini-ch/reykjanes-sar-analysis); [Yekta](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/yekta/)). Several nominees made unsupported claims, such as "99.7% accuracy", or listed NISAR among their inputs without showing any product ([NARO](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/naro/); [Arkivators](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/find-a-team/arkivators/)).

Two conclusions follow, and they pull in different directions. The SAR record suggests that **radar depth alone does not carry an entry past nominee**. The 2023–24 winner record suggests that **the Science, Data and Technology awards went to working, data-heavy builds**, while concept-only entries took the softer awards. Both can hold at once: depth earns credibility, and presentation and audience decide advancement. With one finalist per SAR year and no judge comments published anywhere, this reading is pattern-matching on very few cases, not causation.

The record also shows **an opening that fits SARtale closely**. No earlier entry convincingly processed real NISAR data. In 2025 NISAR appeared mostly as StoryMap links, and one team's "NISAR sample" filename carries a 2008 acquisition date, which suggests simulated data. **No nominee described a formal per-pixel change test**, since thresholds stood in for tests. Explicit "what SAR cannot see" treatment appeared in about four projects. No 2025 entry combined these in an interactive app with uncertainty shown to the user. The 2026 brief asks for exactly that combination: an "interactive application" and an "open-source, user-friendly solution" on NISAR data that tracks change "at different time steps". It keeps 2025's line "Hypothesize the physical drivers behind what you see (or don't see)" and adds "What future applications of NISAR are possible?" ([2026 challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/dancing-with-the-sars/)). Competition will be heavier. **605 teams had registered by 2 November 2026, about twice 2025's 306** ([2026 teams](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/dancing-with-the-sars/?tab=teams)).

## Specificity, completeness and award fit separate winners from good entries

The published evidence supports a standard, not a formula. No 2023–2025 judges' citations, scores or comments exist. The closest material is NASA's one-line award definitions and editorial blurbs. Finalist and Honorable Mention pools were not analysed in any note, so there is no matched sample of near-misses. Within those limits, three differences stand out.

**First, a precise question with a visible outcome.** The good SAR entries that did not win already had real data and often a named place. Codex makes the useful point that FarmHubGlobal and Cea-SAR (a local first place and Global Nominee) also described a specific user, so **a clear audience is necessary but not sufficient** ([Codex review](<../research_notes/Space Apps winners vs SARtale/codex_independent_review.md>); [Cea-SAR organiser account](https://hackathlone.com/blogs/inside-the-2025-podium)). Codex sets out the step-up from good to strong as a table: from "a working visualization" to "a precise question the visualization helps someone answer", and from "many disclaimers" to "uncertainty that helps the visitor make the right interpretation". The SAR and winner notes support that reading, though they cannot prove it.

**Second, completeness that matches the award.** The 2024 guide's Relevance criterion asks, "Is it a complete project or does it have a long way to go?", and its Validity criterion asks, "Can it work in the real world?" ([2024 Judging & Awards Guide, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116173700/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/)). That explains why Figma, Canva and comic entries won concept or heart awards and never Science or Data. The same guide tells teams to give **Presentation equal attention**: "devoting time and resources to developing your pitch and building a great story around your project."

**Third, challenge-to-award fit.** In 2025, the Storytelling and Art & Technology winners answered challenges that asked for a children's story and an educational game. Three winners came from the one challenge that asked for a business model. For a data-storytelling app, the notes agree that the best fits are **Best Use of Storytelling and Best Use of Data**, with Science possible. SpaceGenes+ won Science on a simple, transparent statistic. Local Impact needs a real local user, which SARtale does not yet have. Codex reaches the same conclusion independently. Whether teams nominate an award or judges assign one could not be confirmed from any guide.

Expert teams also compete. A company (Nave Analytics) won in 2024, as did a university research group (WMPGang) and a company-backed team (Spacebee) in 2023. But team size does not decide outcomes. **Winners ran from one person to six** in 2025, and UpIT reached global nomination with two developers.

## SARtale earns validity points and loses them on impact and delivery

The audit scores SARtale, by its own judgement, at **Impact 2, Creativity 3.5, Validity 4, Relevance 4.5 and Presentation 2.5 out of 5**, adding that "the app alone would earn 4, but no judge can reach it yet" ([audit](<../research_notes/Space Apps winners vs SARtale/sartale_audit.md>)). The app is a polished single-area story on **NISAR L2 GCOV provisional data from 9 dates, 2 July to 18 October 2026, over the Peshawar Valley and Tarbela/Attock** ([manifest](../../app/public/data/manifest.json); [DATA_SOURCES](../DATA_SOURCES.md)). It has a ten-step tour, "The Dance" full-screen frame, a date-coloured change map, seven evidence-labelled site cards, a "What NISAR Missed" coverage view, and a 1,832-word "How we know" page. Its controls include shuffled-date tests, an OPERA comparison and a published list of "what would change our mind". Measured against the winners above, these are real strengths. "What NISAR Missed" answers the brief's "(or don't see)" line more directly than any earlier SAR entry did. **The change map is the visual a pitch should lead with.**

The weaknesses map almost one to one onto the gaps between winners and good entries. Neither the start screen nor the story names a user. The only "use" content is a final card saying a planner "could" check flagged river stretches. No stakeholder has been consulted, and the planned decision ending and printable field-check list were not built ([story.json](../../app/src/content/story.json); [plan](../SARtale-hackathon-plan.md)). The story mentions no people, villages, roads or bridges. It never says that **Tarbela is an operated reservoir**, which is the obvious "human driver" the brief invites. Future applications, which the brief asks about explicitly, are a list of data to add. Delivery is worse still. **There is no deployed app, the repo appears to be private** (anonymous 404) **with `build/sartale` unmerged and one commit unpushed, and there is no deck and no hosted video** ([audit](<../research_notes/Space Apps winners vs SARtale/sartale_audit.md>)). The challenge requires an open-source solution, so a private repo is a Relevance problem as well as a Presentation one.

The audit and Codex agree on the main science caveats, which come from the project's own documents and were not recomputed by either reviewer. **1.39% of pixels are flagged against a shuffled-order maximum of 1.26%.** The three controls were used to set the threshold, and one control was swapped after failing. Two plain sites flag at 35% and 68% with no known cause. **Only 24% of flags survive every single-date drop.** OPERA has no tile between 28 June and 15 September, so nothing external checks the August step ([DATA_SOURCES](../DATA_SOURCES.md); [evidence.json](../../app/src/content/evidence.json)). Both reviewers single out the app's "independent reviewer" wording. The audit calls it "the single riskiest phrase in the app", because the evidence suggests an AI review agent in the same workflow and the five-city check has no script or city names in the repo ([sitesView.ts](../../app/src/sitesView.ts); [SI_USE](../SI_USE.md)). Codex asks for "rerunnable evidence or remove it". Codex adds a point the audit lacks. The hero's "Around 19 Aug" reads as an observed onset, when the honest statement is an **observation interval of 26 July to 19 August**. Codex also notes that the evidence page wrongly says only the first listed source is satellite data, although OPERA is also satellite-derived.

Where the two reviews differ, the differences matter for planning:

- **Mobile and loading.** The audit measured an emulated phone at 390×844. Story text overflowed the card on all ten steps, the change panel covered the map, and the landing page fetched 15 to 17 MB unprompted, taking about 96 seconds to load all frames at 1.6 Mbps. Codex, which ran nothing, calls mobile "an untested delivery risk, not evidence that the app currently fails on phones", and counts 34.4 MB of frame files, which is the inventory and not the initial download. **The audit's emulated measurement is the stronger evidence.** Neither review tested a real phone.
- **More science versus no more layers.** The audit proposes a DEM check of the shore against the 1528 to 1550 ft reservoir levels, HLS NDVI and IMERG rain for the unexplained 19 August plains flags, and possibly track 012 (about 66 GB). Codex says "More layers will not fix that" and steers away from large new downloads, though its own fix 3 asks for a small, freshly preselected evaluation. Both positions hold up. A DEM check is cheap and directly tests the headline claim. Track 012 is not worth its cost in a weekend.
- **Who the user is.** The audit frames Impact around planners and notes that road closures, now deferred, were the plan's only route to it. Codex recommends **a reachable educational audience**, local geography students or teachers, and a measured learning task, the "Radar Jury". It warns that calling the build a decision tool "would create expectations its observations and validation cannot meet". Given that no planner has been contacted, Codex's framing is the safer one.
- **Scope wording.** Codex writes that its brief framed the project as "2025–26 and Peshawar–Charsadda–Nowshera", while the repo holds one 2026 season with no Charsadda or Nowshera analysis site. The audit describes only the 2026 season. The conflict lies between Codex's prompt and the repo, not between the reviews. The plan also records "early-warning tool" wording on the team page that must change ([plan](../SARtale-hackathon-plan.md)). **Any pitch, page or deck text implying two monsoons or an early-warning capability should be removed.**

The two reviews agree on the gate. The plan rates the early-start risk Severe, saying a Local Lead objection "may mean discarding this work". All 61 commits are dated 1–2 November 2026, and the Local Lead's reply is not on record ([SI_USE](../SI_USE.md); [plan](../SARtale-hackathon-plan.md)). Codex is careful to say it "does not determine eligibility". So is this report.

7. If hours remain: phone layout, then the evidence panel, then the DEM check.

## Conclusion

The winner record changes what "doing better" means for SARtale. Its distinctive asset is not a feature still to be built. It is a combination no SAR entry has shown: real NISAR data, a tested change layer, and an honest account of what the radar missed. That combination is currently hidden behind a methods-first video, a private repo and an ending that hands the visitor a hypothetical planner. Past SAR finalists advanced with far less science and far clearer stakes. The cheapest gains therefore come from saying less, earlier and more concretely, not from adding layers.

The open question is less about judging than about rules. The eligibility ruling, and the 13 November guides that will confirm or replace the inferred 30-second and seven-slide limits, decide which version of this plan is legal at all. Until both are in hand, the safest framing is the one Codex proposes, which the audit's evidence supports. SARtale should be an investigation in which the visitor learns when radar evidence supports an interpretation, when it is suspect, and when it is simply missing. It should not be pitched as flood intelligence that its single track and nine dates cannot yet deliver.
