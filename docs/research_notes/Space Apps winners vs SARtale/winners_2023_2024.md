Retrieval notes (read first):

## Q1. Which projects won each global award in 2024 and in 2023?

### Takeaway
Both years used the same ten global awards. In 2024, 6 of 10 winners were Earth-science or Earth-observation products, two of them on a single agriculture challenge. In 2023 the mix was broader: 2 of 10 winners were lunar seismology projects on one challenge, and the rest spread across space weather, oceans, water, eclipses, sonification, a comic, and imaging spectroscopy.

### Inferences
- The award set did not change between 2023 and 2024, so award-to-award comparisons across the two years are like for like.
- A single challenge produced two winners in each year: Moonquake Map 2.0 in 2023 and the agriculture EO challenge in 2024. Challenges with rich, well-documented NASA datasets seem to yield several strong entries.

### Gaps
- The global-winners pages on spaceappschallenge.org render their winner lists client-side. Archived copies hold only award metadata and opaque team IDs, so the lists above rest on the NASA articles plus each team's archived project page.
- The 2023 announcement date: the NASA article is dated 26 Jan 2024. A secondary search summary said the live event was 23 Jan 2024 at Goddard, but I did not verify that.
- LunarTech Ensemble's location differs by source: NASA says "Egypt", while the project page's location field says "Universal Event / United States". Both are recorded above.

## Q2. Per-winner evidence: product, form, data, method, demo, repo, citation

### Takeaway
Most winners in both years were working web apps with a public URL, a 30-second YouTube demo, and a GitHub repo. The exceptions were mostly concept or prototype entries that won the softer awards: Best Mission Concept 2024 (a Figma prototype), Most Inspirational 2023 (Canva), and Art & Technology 2023 (a comic). "Judges' citations" exist only as NASA's one- or two-sentence summaries; I found no judge-authored citations.

### Cited Findings
Unless noted, each entry's facts come from the team's archived project page, linked at the start of the entry. "NASA blurb" is the summary sentence in the NASA announcement for that year. These blurbs are the closest thing to a published citation.

#### 2024

**WMPGang: SkyShield / NEO Orrery (Best Use of Science)** — [project page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183832/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/wmpgang/?tab=project)
- What it does: a physically based interactive 3D orrery web app. It shows 8 planets, 5 dwarf planets, 1,669 NEOs from the ESA risk list, 34 showers with 107 meteoroid streams, and the sporadic meteoroid complex. Users can filter by semi-major axis, eccentricity, diameter, and Palermo rating, and control time. Meteor stream orbits appear only when the stream is active near Earth.
- Team: from Western University's Western Meteor Physics Group (WMPG), and specialists in meteor streams, NEOs, and satellite risk.
- Form: web app (JavaScript/HTML, Three.js; Python preprocessing). Live link: simonvse.com/projects/neo-orrery/app/.
- Data: ESA NEO risk list and downloads, the IAU Meteor Data Center stream data, and JPL SBDB for planets and parent bodies. Most of the data is partner or non-NASA (ESA, IAU), not NASA.
- Demo: YouTube "NEO Orrery Demo", 93 s (1:33) — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RBl_3ZsmVFo)
- Repo: github.com/SimonVanSchuylenbergh/NEO-Orrery. Exists, JavaScript, 10 stars, last push 2024-10-07 — [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/SimonVanSchuylenbergh/NEO-Orrery)
- AI declared: ChatGPT for HTML/UI snippets and debugging.
- NASA blurb: "created a web app that identifies satellite risk zones using real-time data on Near-Earth Objects and meteor streams" — [NASA 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)

**GaamaRamma: WaterWise (Best Use of Data)** — [project page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183805/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/?tab=project)
- What it does: farmers draw field polygons on a map and enter crop type, growth stage, and irrigation efficiency. The app returns a 7-day irrigation schedule. A dashboard labels each field "Hydrated" or "Dehydrated" and tracks "Hydration Jobs". "Sprout AI" is a GPT-3.5-turbo chatbot.
- Method: Water Usage = (crop coefficient × ET − precipitation) × area. Area comes from the Shoelace formula with an Earth-curvature correction; run time = water ÷ flow rate, with irrigation scheduled for 10 PM.
- Data: OpenET evapotranspiration (NASA-supported), Open-Meteo precipitation, a crop-coefficient index; NASA Worldview and Earthdata Search are also listed.
- Form: web app (Next.js/TypeScript, Leaflet/OpenStreetMap, on Vercel). Live link: nasa-hackathon-2024-nine.vercel.app. A Figma prototype is also linked.
- Demo: YouTube, 31 s, titled "WaterWise (NASA Space Apps Challenge 2024 - Global Winner for Best Use of Data)" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wg-gE3kYaas)
- Repo: github.com/ggalmeida0/nasa-hackathon-2024. Exists, TypeScript, 6 stars — [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/ggalmeida0/nasa-hackathon-2024)
- NASA blurb: "aimed to create a sustainable way to help farmers efficiently manage water availability in the face of drought, pests, and disease" — [NASA 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)

**42 QuakeHeroes (Best Use of Technology)** — [project page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116175928/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/42-quakeheroes/?tab=project)
- What it does: detects quake onsets in Apollo and Mars InSight ground-velocity data.
- Method: Butterworth band-pass filtering, then Empirical Mode Decomposition into IMFs, then sliding-window energy features forming a 2D matrix. A U-Net encoder-decoder (PyTorch) is trained against a Gaussian label centred on arrival time. Training used 63 lunar cases with 13 held out for validation, and the 2 Mars cases for validation only. Settings: AdamW (lr 0.001), ReduceLROnPlateau, MSE loss, 50 epochs. Self-reported accuracy within ±0.1 was 96.18% on Moon data and 91.31% on Mars data. These figures are the team's claim and were not independently checked.
- Form: ML model plus an immersive web page (gabrielchayb.github.io/42-quake-heroes/).
- Data: the challenge's Apollo and InSight dataset, plus NASA 3D Moon, Mars, and lander models.
- Demo: YouTube "PITCH 2024", 30 s — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=thxLntBLF1I)
- Repos: github.com/gabrielchayb/42-model-source-notebook (Jupyter, 12 stars) and github.com/gabrielchayb/42-quake-heroes (4 stars) — [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/gabrielchayb/42-model-source-notebook)
- AI declared: ChatGPT 3.5, GitHub Copilot, and DALL-E (for the logo).
- NASA blurb: "employed a deep neural network model to identify the precise locations of seismic events within time-series data" — [NASA 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)

**NVS-knot: 2plant | ! 2plant (Galactic Impact)** — [project page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183831/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/nvs-knot/?tab=project)
- What it does: a pre-planting soil-moisture risk service. It answers two questions: "Is soil moisture sufficient for germination?" and "Is it safe to bring equipment?" It produces daily sufficiency and saturation risk scores for the top 15 cm at 100 m resolution, at least 2 weeks ahead, plus a "planting window" timeline (poor, fair, good, best) and a "second opinion" tab.
- Team: a joint entry from an existing company, Nave Analytics, with the VITech and Sparrow teams. It was framed as an MVP of a commercial B2B product, with a pilot price of $0.50 per acre and a TAM analysis ("$125M").
- Method: a reformulated recursive exponential filter projects SMAP and AMSR2 surface moisture into the root zone. It assimilates Meteomatics forecast ensembles and uses SETMI thermal ET models (OLEM, TSM, SEBAL) with Landsat and Sentinel-2 inputs, plus uncertainty propagation.
- Data: SMAP L4 via earthaccess, MODIS/Aqua MYD21A1D LST, and ECOSTRESS L2 LST; partner data from Meteomatics via EOSDA.
- Form: web app (AWS, Cognito, React, Java; Python/xarray/plotly dashboards; HTTP API with JSON/CSV export). Live link: nasa.nave-app.com/list-view. Demo fields were in Ukraine, the USA, and Argentina.
- Demo: Google Drive video (length not verified).
- Repo: github.com/yaroslav-prokipchyn/2plantORNOT2plant. Exists, Python, 1 star, single push 2024-10-06 — [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/yaroslav-prokipchyn/2plantORNOT2plant)
- NASA blurb: "assessed planting conditions using surface soil moisture and evapotranspiration data, then created an app that empowers farmers to manage planting risks" — [NASA 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)

**Storm Prophet (Best Use of Data)** — [project page](https://web.archive.org/web/20240125104441/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/storm-prophet/?tab=project)
- What it does: predicts the Dst geomagnetic storm index from raw DSCOVR L1 data (2016–2023), with a short-term model (up to 14 h, per-minute) and a long-term model (up to 30 days, hourly).
- Method: the team cut 54 columns to 8 engineered features. The model is a sequence-to-sequence LSTM with dropout and L2 regularisation, early stopping, Adam, MSE loss, and permutation feature importance. A reverse LSTM trained on 2017–2020 flags Faraday-cup anomalies in 2020–2023. Self-reported deviations: 1.8% (short-term) and 3% (long-term). These were not checked.

The no-code winners (Canva game, comic, sonification method, Figma concept) all took softer awards (Most Inspirational, Art & Technology, Global Connection, Best Mission Concept). Best Use of Science, Data, and Technology went to working, data-heavy builds in both years.
- **Who entered.** Existing organisations or companies won in both years: Nave Analytics with partners in 2024, Spacebee in 2023, and a university meteor-physics research group (WMPGang) in 2024. Expert-team entries clearly compete.
- **Partner data.** Partner and non-NASA data appears often: ESA NEO data, IAU meteor data, WDC Kyoto Dst, Meteomatics, UAE government data, and AQICN. Partner data does not seem to hurt.

## Q3. Earth-observation / map-based apps and data-storytelling products (closest analogues)

### Takeaway
2024 is where the closest analogues sit. Six winners were Earth-observation products, and three of them (WaterWise, 2plant, G.R.O.W.) were map-centred decision tools built on a single key NASA EO product: OpenET, SMAP, and FIRMS respectively. Two 2024 winners (TerraTales, Connected Earth Museum) and two 2023 winners (Greetings from Earth, Aqua Explorer) were data-storytelling products. They won on narrative devices (characters, an alien, a museum host) layered over a few NASA datasets, not on analytical depth. The only true imaging-science remote-sensing method among the 20 winners was 2023's EMIT hotspot finder. No winner used SAR.

### Cited Findings
**Earth-observation or map-based winners**
- **WaterWise (2024, Best Use of Data)**: OpenET ET on Leaflet field polygons, turned into an actionable 7-day schedule. The page says it focuses "on actions rather than raw numbers" and labels fields "Hydrated" or "Dehydrated" — [GaamaRamma page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183805/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/?tab=project)

- **G.R.O.W. (2024, Local Impact; "Community Mapping" challenge)**: FIRMS wildfire data through a voice/LLM map agent, motivated by one named farmer's loss — [Team I.O. page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183757/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/team-io/?tab=project). The challenge asked teams to "create a map that incorporates open science data to explore how an issue in your community is shaped by the surrounding physical geography" (same page, challenge excerpt).

The "Tell Us a Climate Story!" challenge required the U.S. Greenhouse Gas Center data — [TerraTales page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183834/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/terratales/?tab=project)

- **Aqua Explorer (2023, Best Storytelling)**: a gated learn-then-play level structure with a country-choice map. ### Inferences
- **What carries an EO app.** The EO decision tools that won converted one EO variable into a plain-language action or status: water schedule, planting window, fire count. None of them showed raw rasters as the endpoint.
- **How storytelling winners were built.** They used a character or role frame (Mother Earth, an alien recipient, a museum host, child protagonists), a linear or chaptered path, and an interactive check at the end (game or quiz). They drew on few datasets (one to four) and kept the analysis simple. TerraTales' CNN-LSTM forecast was the most technical piece among them.
- **Personal stakes.** Local Impact (G.R.O.W.) leaned on a named individual case study ("Gabriel's farm"), with dates and dollar losses tied to dated FIRMS detections.
- **Gap for SARtale.** None of the 20 winners used SAR or InSAR, so the winners offer no direct precedent to emulate or avoid on that front.

### Gaps
- The 2024 Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions lists, where other EO and storytelling entries may sit, were not pulled. The archived pages exist (CDX shows /nasa-space-apps-2024/awards/global-finalists/), but Wayback began rate-limiting (HTTP 429).
- How much "real NASA satellite data integration" Aqua Explorer actually had is not verifiable from the page.

## Q4. What were the judging criteria in those years?

### Takeaway
For 2024 the criteria are verified: five equally weighted-sounding standards (Influence, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation) applied in a three-phase process, plus a one-line definition for each of the ten awards. I could not retrieve 2023-specific criteria text. The 2023 judging process is confirmed only at the level of "subject matter experts from NASA and 13 Space Agency Partners".

### Cited Findings
- 2024 criteria, verbatim, from the [Judging & Awards Guide (archived 16 Jan 2025)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116173700/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/):
  - "Influence: How much influence (quality and quantity) can this project have? Does it address a big problem or a little problem? Will it inspire or help many, or a few?"
  - "Creativity: How creative/innovative is the approach? Is the project novel and something that hasn't been attempted before, or is it an incremental improvement on something that already exists?"
  - "Validity: Is the proposed solution scientifically valid? Will it do what it sets out to do? Can it work in the real world?"
  - "Relevance: Is this project responsive to the challenge for which it was submitted? Is it a complete project or does it have a long way to go? Is it technically feasible? How usable or user-friendly is the proposed solution?"
  - "Presentation: How well did the team communicate their project? Were they effective in telling the story of the project: the challenge, the proposed solution, and why is it important?"
  - The guide adds: "remember to pay equal attention to the final criterion: presentation. We recommend devoting time and resources to developing your pitch and building a great story around your project."
- 2024 process: Phase 1, local judges (or SMEs for the Universal Event, "usually around one Global Nominee per ten projects submitted") pick Global Nominees. Phase 2, NASA and partner expert judges pick Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions. Phase 3, an executive committee of "agency leaders and executives" evaluates each finalist and assigns the ten awards — [Judging & Awards Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116173700/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/)
- 2024 award definitions (verbatim, same guide):
  - Best Use of Science: "best and most valid use of the scientific method"
  - Best Use of Data: "best makes space data accessible, or leverages it to a unique application"
  - Best Use of Technology: "most innovative use of technology"
  - Galactic Impact: "most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe"
  - Best Mission Concept: "most plausible concept and design"
  - Most Inspirational: "captures our hearts"
  - Global Connection: "best connects people around the world through technology"
  - Art & Technology: "most effectively combines technical and creative skills"
  - Local Impact: "greatest potential for making a difference in their local community"
- 2024 eligibility rules that shape submissions: answer an official challenge ("Create Your Own Challenge" is not eligible); 1–6 members; English on the page and in linked materials (a demo video may be in another language with English subtitles); list all external resources, AI included; PRC-affiliation review — [Judging & Awards Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116173700/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/)
- 2023: winners were "the highest rated projects out of 5,556 submissions, as determined by subject matter experts from NASA and 13 Space Agency Partners" — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners)
- The project-submission form in both years had a separate AI-use field (`aiReferences`) alongside data and project references, as seen on every archived 2023 and 2024 project page, for example [Storm Prophet 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20240125104441/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/storm-prophet/?tab=project)

### Inferences
- The Relevance criterion asks "Is it a complete project or does it have a long way to go?" and Validity asks "Can it work in the real world?". Together these explain why the science, data, and technology awards went to working builds, while concept-only entries landed in concept or heart awards.
- The award set was identical in 2023 and 2024, and the guide's URL is undated and evergreen (/resources/judging-awards-guide/). It is likely, but **unverified**, that 2023 used the same five criteria.

### Gaps
- No 2023 version of the judging guide text was retrieved. The Wayback snapshots dated 2023 redirect to a 17 Jul 2024 version, and the 2023 judging page and awards page snapshots carried no criteria text. The CSA's 2023 participant guide PDF might hold them, but its host has the same TLS root problem and Wayback then returned HTTP 429.
- No scoring weights, rubric sheets, or per-criterion scores for any winner were found.
- The demo-video length rule (apparently 30 s) comes from the Project Submission Guide, which I did not retrieve.

## Q5. What changed between 2023 and 2024?

### Takeaway
2024 was larger (9,996 vs 5,556 submissions) and tilted further toward Earth observation and decision-support tools. LLM features also moved from a side feature in one 2023 winner to a core component in four 2024 winners. Non-code winners fell from three in 2023 to roughly one in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **Scale.** 2023: 57,999 participants, 152 countries and territories, 5,556 submissions — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners). 2024: 93,520 participants, 163 countries and territories, 485 events, 9,996 submissions — [NASA 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)
- **Earth focus.** In 2024, six winners were Earth-science or EO: WaterWise, 2plant, Landsat Connect, TerraTales, Connected Earth Museum, and G.R.O.W., with Eco-Metropolis lightly EO. In 2023, four were Earth-focused: Greetings from Earth (MODIS ocean colour), Aqua Explorer (water missions), the EMIT hotspot finder, and, if counted as Earth-impact data, Storm Prophet's space weather. This count is my classification, based on the challenge excerpts on the archived project pages ([2024 example](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183805/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/?tab=project), [2023 example](https://web.archive.org/web/20240126182152/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/quality_over_quantity/?tab=project))
- **LLMs.** In 2024, four winners had an LLM as a product component: WaterWise (GPT-3.5 "Sprout AI"), G.R.O.W. (an LLM agent that writes SQL and drives the map), Asteroid Destroyer (a ChatGPT habitability classifier), and Connected Earth Museum (an AI host voiced with ElevenLabs). In 2023 only Spacebee shipped one (a GPT chatbot via Zapier), and LunarTech's planned LLM was not integrated — per each team's archived project page ([Team I.O.](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116183757/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/team-io/?tab=project), [Spacebee](https://web.archive.org/web/20240126182153/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/spacebee/?tab=project), [LunarTech](https://web.archive.org/web/20240126182153/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/find-a-team/lunartech-ensemble/?tab=project))
- **ML.** Models appear in both years. 2023 had Storm Prophet's LSTM. 2024 had 42 QuakeHeroes' U-Net and TerraTales' CNN-LSTM — per project pages above.
- **Non-code winners.** 2023 had three: Space Quest Maidens (Canva), Oogway Comics (comic), and Arcobaleno (a sonification method with Drive media). 2024 had one: Landsat Connect (a Figma concept), with Eco-Metropolis a Pygame build — per project pages above.
- **Repos.** 8 of 10 2024 winners and 6 of 10 2023 winners linked a public GitHub repo; Landsat Connect and TerraTales listed none in 2024. All 16 repos checked still exist as of 2 Nov 2026 — [GitHub API checks](https://api.github.com/repos/Wizard2007/storm-prophet) (one query per repo; full list in Q2).
- **Demo length.** The ~30 s YouTube demo norm holds in both years (Q2 inference).

### Inferences
- From 2023 to 2024 the centre of gravity moved toward "EO data turned into a decision or alert for a specific user" (farmers in three winners). That fits Space Apps' funding by NASA's Earth Science Division, which the guide footer notes — [Judging & Awards Guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20250116173700/https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/). It also fits a 2024 challenge slate that included several applied-EO challenges.

### Gaps
- I did not count the full challenge lists for each year, so whether the EO shift reflects the challenge mix or judges' preferences cannot be separated.
- Finalist and honorable-mention pools were not analysed, so these trends rest on n = 10 per year.
