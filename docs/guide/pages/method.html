The method

SARtale uses plain statistics on radar backscatter: a darkness rule, a two-direction shadow rule and a change test against each lake's own noise. No AI model runs in the pipeline or the app.

The data

SARtale reads NASA's NISAR Level-2 GCOV product (geocoded, polarimetric covariance, about 10 m pixels) from the ASF archive. All passes in this build are from the provisional release, CRID P05023, which NASA has not validated and will reprocess. It uses two polarisations: HH (sent and received horizontally) and HV (sent horizontally, received vertically).

Six steps, per lake and per pass

  1. Find passesSearch NASA's CMR catalogue for NISAR GCOV granules whose footprint covers the lake (lakes-search).
  2. Read a windowRead a 6 km × 6 km window around the lake straight from the archive with HTTP range requests, never the whole file (lakes-fetch). Windows are cached in data/lakes/.
  3. Find dark waterAverage the linear power in a 3 × 3 pixel box to calm the speckle, convert to decibels, then mark every pixel at least 3 dB below the window median in both HH and HV. Calm water reflects radar away from the satellite, so it looks dark.
  4. Remove shadowA slope facing away from the satellite falls in radar shadow and looks as dark as water. NISAR sees each lake heading north (ascending) and heading south (descending). From each direction's season median, a pixel dark from only one direction is shadow and is set aside; a lake is dark from both.
  5. MeasureCount the remaining dark pixels inside the lake's zone and convert to km². The zone is the lake's outline buffered by 200 m where there is one (Attabad, from OpenStreetMap), otherwise a 1 km circle around a published coordinate.
  6. DecideCompare the latest valid area only with earlier valid passes from the same track, since a different viewing angle sees a lake differently. The change counts as Growing or Shrinking only if it is more than twice the lake's own noise.

Noise and the change test

For the track with the newest valid pass, take the earlier areas a₁ … aₙ₋₁ (not the latest, so a real change cannot widen its own threshold):

  • Reference: the median of the earlier areas.
  • Noise: 1.4826 × the median of the absolute pass-to-pass differences, at least 0.005 km². With fewer than three earlier areas the noise is the floor.
  • Change: the latest area minus the reference. Growing if change > 2 × noise, Shrinking if change < −2 × noise, otherwise Stable.

Worked example. Attabad on 23 October 2026, track 156A: 2.082 km² against a median of 1.834 km² from 6 earlier looks. The change is +0.248 km² and the noise 0.520 km², so the threshold is 1.040 km² and the lake reads Stable.

Passes that are left out

A pass is excluded, with its reason shown in the app, when:

  • its window-median HH is more than 3 dB from its track's median (too bright or too dark overall);
  • on a track with at least 4 passes, its dark share or window-median HV is an outlier: more than 3.5 robust z-scores (median and 1.4826 × MAD) from the track, with floors of 1 percentage point and 1 dB;
  • it is on the manual list in processing/sartale/config.py. In this build that is 2026-09-05_156A, anomalous over the Hunza lakes.

A pass where less than half of the lake zone can be seen is not used for the status either.

Checking ourselves

lakes-check compares radar water with Sentinel-2 water (MNDWI) on days when both saw a lake under a clear sky. At the four lakes checked, the overlap was poor everywhere (IoU 0.013 to 0.09) and the areas differed widely. Neither source is a clean reference, and the app says so. The full numbers are in lakes.json under checks.

Known limits

  • Lakes whose shores sit in radar shadow are under-counted.
  • Frozen lakes, lakes under 0.05 km² and wind-roughened water are not validated.
  • Only 72% of Attabad's outline falls inside the 6 km window, so its area is the part inside the window.
  • A 1 km circle around a published coordinate can include land, or miss part of a large lake.
