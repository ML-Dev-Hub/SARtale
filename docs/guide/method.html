The method

SARtale uses plain statistics on radar backscatter: a darkness rule, a two-direction shadow rule and a change test against each lake's own noise. No AI model runs in the pipeline or the app.

The data#

SARtale reads NASA's NISAR Level-2 GCOV product (geocoded, polarimetric covariance, about 10 m pixels) from the ASF archive. Passes up to 21 Oct 2026 are from the provisional release, CRID P05023, which NASA has not validated and will reprocess. Later passes are NASA's standard GCOV product, CRID P05036; we have no statement that it is validated. Each pass keeps its own CRID, and a lake whose status track mixes the two carries a limit, because a calibration change between them can shift areas. It uses two polarisations: HH (sent and received horizontally) and HV (sent horizontally, received vertically).

Six steps, per lake and per pass#

  1. Find passesSearch NASA's CMR catalogue for NISAR GCOV granules whose footprint covers the lake (lakes-search).
  2. Read a windowRead a 6 km × 6 km window around the lake straight from the archive with HTTP range requests, never the whole file (lakes-fetch). Windows are cached in data/lakes/.
  3. Find dark waterAverage the linear power in a 3 × 3 pixel box to calm the speckle, convert to decibels, then mark every pixel at least 3 dB below the window median in both HH and HV. Calm water reflects radar away from the satellite, so it looks dark.
  4. Remove shadowA slope facing away from the satellite falls in radar shadow and looks as dark as water. NISAR sees each lake heading north (ascending) and heading south (descending). From each direction's season median, a pixel dark from one direction and seen, not dark, from the other is shadow and is set aside; a lake is dark from both. A pixel the other direction did not measure is not called shadow.
  5. MeasureCount the remaining dark pixels inside the lake's zone and convert to km². The zone is the lake's outline buffered by 200 m where there is one (from OpenStreetMap), otherwise a circle around a published coordinate: 1 km, or smaller where the register sets zone_radius_km for a small pond.
  6. DecideCompare the latest valid area only with earlier valid passes from the same track, since a different viewing angle sees a lake differently. The change counts as Growing or Shrinking only if it is more than twice the lake's own noise.

Noise and the change test#

For the track with the newest valid pass, take the earlier areas a₁ … aₙ₋₁ (not the latest, so a real change cannot widen its own threshold):

  • Reference: the median of the earlier areas.
  • Noise: 1.4826 × the median of the absolute pass-to-pass differences, at least 0.005 km². With fewer than three earlier areas the noise is the floor.
  • Change: the latest area minus the reference. Growing if change > 2 × noise, Shrinking if change < −2 × noise, otherwise Stable.
  • New processing version: if the change is past 2 × noise but the latest pass is the first on a processing version (CRID) that none of the earlier passes has, the status is Change not confirmed yet. A second pass on that version joins the baseline, and Growing or Shrinking applies if the change holds. A change within the noise stays Stable.
  • No lake now: a site the register marks lake_state: "no_lake" (recent imagery shows no lake, such as a drained ice-dammed lake's basin) gets this status whatever its dark area does. Its passes are still read and listed, but there is no change, no noise and no water layer. Each refresh, all year, re-checks the site with Sentinel-2: the newest scene whose lake zone is at least 80% clear of cloud, shadow, snow and ice, with snow or ice on no more than 1% of it (a frozen or snow-covered pond reads as dry ground), and with pixels within 100 m of cloud or cloud shadow not counted as clear (their edges read as water). It records the scene, its date and the water and snow shares of the zone. When no scene qualifies, the last check stands and the status says that no clear, snow-free view has come since. When the two most recent qualifying scenes, at least 5 days apart, both show water above the site's threshold (4% of the zone by default; set per site where a river or pond shares the zone), the site becomes Lake may be forming: still no verdict, and its radar dark area is shown but not yet treated as lake area; the SARtale team reviews the site to add it as a measured lake. A single scene above the threshold is recorded as pending and confirmed or cleared by the next one; a forming site returns to No lake now only after two qualifying scenes below it.

Worked example (from the lake data built 12 Nov 2026; the guide is rebuilt from lakes.json, and the app always shows the current numbers). Attabad on 9 November 2026, track 055A: 2.640 km² against a median of 2.734 km² from 11 earlier looks. The change is -0.094 km² and the noise 0.120 km², so the threshold is 2 × 0.120 = 0.240 km² and the lake reads Stable.

Frozen lakes#

L-band radar does not see a mountain lake freeze: new ice under dry snow stays as dark as open water, while frozen ground around the lake darkens and reads as more water (at Shazore the area rose by 44% after freeze-up, while Sentinel-2 showed the lake under snow and ice). So lakes-freeze, run after lakes-build at each refresh, decides whether a lake is probably frozen from air temperature and, when one is clear, a Sentinel-2 image; never from the radar area. Only sites the register holds as lakes are checked; a site with no lake now gets no freeze state.

  • Air temperature: NASA POWER daily mean (T2M) and minimum (T2M_MIN) at the lake from 1 Sep, moved from the POWER grid cell's height to the lake's at −6.5 °C per km. The lake's height is the 10th percentile of the Copernicus DEM GLO-30 within 1 km of the lake point, measured once with lakes-elevation (the rebuild runs it before the build for any lake without one, so a newly accepted lake gets it in the same refresh) and stored in lakes_in.json. Without it the freeze is not assessed.
  • Frozen (likely): the daily mean below 0 °C on at least 7 days in a row and at least 30 freezing degree-days (the sum of −T on days below 0 °C) since 1 Sep. A missing day ends the run. The rule is late rather than early: one to two weeks late where the air hovers around 0 °C (Rogheli, October 2026).
  • Frozen (seen by Sentinel-2): for a cold lake (frozen by the rule, or below 0 °C on the newest day, or with nights below 0 °C over the last week), the clearest scene of the last 7 days whose lake zone is at least 80% clear of cloud, cloud shadow and their 100 m edge; snow or ice on at least 80% of the clear zone. Water on at least 50% of the lake itself instead returns the lake to the usual assessment ("open water seen by Sentinel-2"): of its unbuffered outline where it has one, else of its summer radar water core (the zone pixels dark in at least half of a track's open-water-season passes), counting only the pixels seen clear. Measured over the whole zone, shore included, even an open lake reads far under 50%.
  • Break-up likely: a frozen lake with at least 7 days in a row above 0 °C, or a radar pass whose lake core (the zone pixels dark in at least half of the track's open-water-season passes) is at least 2 dB brighter against its window than in summer in both HH and HV, with a dark share at least 0.3 lower. The test uses core-minus-window contrast only, so a processing-version change cannot trigger it; with fewer than 3 summer passes on a track it is not run there. The lake is open again after 14 warm days in a row and one radar pass back within its summer range (contrast within 1 dB, dark share no more than 0.15 below), or a clear Sentinel-2 image of open water.
  • Each pass gets a frozen flag from the same rule on its own date. Frozen passes keep their area but are left out of the baseline and the noise, and the app greys them. A lake frozen today has the frozen status even when its last pass came before freeze-up.
  • The frozen statuses come before every status but No lake now, Change not confirmed yet included: no Growing or Shrinking verdict, and the area is shown greyed as not an open-water measurement.
  • Freeze-up is called only between 1 Sep and 31 May, but a lake that is frozen or breaking up stays so after 31 May until the break-up and open-again rules release it: a high lake's ice can go out in late June or July (Shazore, 2026). Freezing degree-days start again each 1 Sep; a lake still frozen then stays frozen and says so. When the newest POWER day is more than 10 days old, or POWER cannot be reached, the freeze is not assessed and the usual status stands.
  • Limits: winter radar is untested (the data so far end just after freeze-up), so the rule is re-tested with late-January data; the air temperature is a reanalysis estimate for a cell about 50 km across with one lapse rate; and nothing here says how thick ice is.

Passes that are left out#

A pass is excluded, with its reason shown in the app, when:

  • its window-median HH is more than 3 dB from its track's median (too bright or too dark overall);
  • on a track with at least 4 passes, its dark share or window-median HV is an outlier: more than 3.5 robust z-scores (median and 1.4826 × MAD) from the track, with floors of 1 percentage point and 1 dB;
  • it is on the manual list in processing/sartale/config.py. In this build that is 2026-09-05_156A, anomalous over the Hunza lakes.

A pass where less than half of the lake zone can be seen is not used for the status either.

Checking ourselves#

lakes-check compares radar water with Sentinel-2 water (MNDWI) on days when both saw a lake under a clear sky, at every lake that holds a lake now. It uses only pairs in the open-water season (OPEN_WATER_SEASON in config.py, 15 June to 30 September), the clearest one per lake: in October and November a lake can be frozen or under snow, which optical water never counts, so such a pair is no test of radar water. A lake with no clear in-season pair gets a note instead of a number. Neither source is a clean reference: radar under-reads small lakes and loses water marked shadow-prone, and optical water misses shaded or very dark lakes. The app words the agreement from the IoU values of the current build, in How we know and in each lake's panel; the full numbers are in lakes.json under checks.

Earth Search marks Sentinel-2 scenes whose pixel values already have the bottom-of-atmosphere offset applied (earthsearch:boa_offset_applied). For those scenes lakes-check uses an offset of 0; applying the −0.1 offset a second time had pushed turbid lakes below the water threshold and made the earlier checks look far worse than they were.

Known limits#

  • Lakes whose shores sit in radar shadow are under-counted.
  • Frozen lakes, lakes under 0.05 km² and wind-roughened water are not validated. The frozen status comes from air temperature and Sentinel-2, not from radar; winter radar is untested.
  • Only 69% of Attabad's outline falls inside the 6 km window, so its area is the part inside the window. (Lake data built 12 Nov 2026.)
  • A 1 km circle around a published coordinate can include land, or miss part of a large lake. Lakes with an outline are measured inside it.
  • A site with no lake now (status No lake now) gets no verdict; its dark radar area is ice, slope or river, not a lake.
